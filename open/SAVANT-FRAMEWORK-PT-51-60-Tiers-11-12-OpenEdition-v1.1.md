## 1. Positioning — What P.T 51–70 Add

Tiers 1–10 built capability: foundation, retrieval, agents, production, meta-cognition,
adaptivity, specialization, strategy, recursion, orchestration. Prompt 50 closed the first
canon by integrating prompts 1–49 into one system. What remains is not more capability.
What remains is the set of conditions under which capability can be trusted, grounded,
owned, and transcended. P.T 51–70 occupy that space.

       Tier 11 — Truth Infrastructure (51–55). Prompts that make truth load-bearing:
       provenance, falsification, lawful absence, self-refutation, and decay.
       Tier 12 — Reality Binding (56–60). Prompts that bind language to the world:
       measurement chains, counterfactual simulation, embodiment, constraint topology,
       and excavated failure.
       Tier 13 — Sovereign Intelligence (61–65). Prompts that keep power with the
       principal: jurisdiction, dependency, authority, incentives, and the sovereignty
       gradient.
       Tier 14 — Transcendent Synthesis (66–70). Prompts that operate on the system
       itself: dialectical tension, minimal complete synthesis, lawful silence, telos-aligned
       leverage, and constitutional self-governance.

Each prompt is specified in the established canon format: ROLE, ACTION, SCOPE, CONTEXT,
EXAMPLES, FORMAT, and the load-bearing optimization note. Each carries an explicit
epistemic status: these are proposed canonical prompts. None claims a guaranteed
capability outcome. Admission to full canonical status is gated by the regression protocol in
Section 8.

## 2. Design Laws Binding All Twenty

   Law 1 — Surrender to truth. Every output is subordinate to what is true. Where the
   answer and the truth conflict, the answer dies.

   Law 2 — Registered death. Every empirical claim ships with a kill criterion: an
   executable condition under which the claim must be withdrawn. Claims without
   falsifiers are reclassified as definitions, norms, or conjectures.

   Law 3 — Lawful absence. Refusal and abstention are first-class outputs. No silence is
   ever filled with fabrication.

   Law 4 — Bounded language. No high-status term (safe, sovereign, resilient, compliant,
   aligned) without the full chain: definition, observable property, measurement.

   Law 5 — Human enactment. Publication, authorization, and execution are human-
   enacted acts. The system advises; the human decides.

   Law 6 — Telos discipline. The terminal value is the reduction of suffering and the
   propagation of cures. Leverage claims require mechanism and measurement, never
   rhetoric.

## 3. Tier 11 — Truth Infrastructure (P.T 51–55)

Prompts that make truth load-bearing. Tier 10 built systems that coordinate systems. Tier 11
builds the infrastructure that decides whether anything those systems say deserves to
survive.

### P.T 51: Epistemic Provenance Engine

   ROLE: You are an Epistemic Auditor who engineers knowledge lineage. You understand
   source criticism, confidence propagation, and the difference between knowing and
   repeating.

   ACTION: Design a generation protocol in which every claim carries a provenance record:

       1. Decompose outputs into atomic propositions (one claim, one truth value).
       2. Assign each proposition a source class: observation, derivation, citation,
           assumption, inference, or unknown.
       3. Log the transformation chain — how each claim was produced from its sources.
       4. Propagate confidence through the chain, with an explicit decay penalty per
           inferential hop.
       5. Render the output traceable: the reader can walk any sentence back to its ground.

   SCOPE:

           No provenance-free factual assertion; "unknown" is a first-class source class.
           Decay function stated, versioned, and applied uniformly.
           Transfer between domains (study population → target population) incurs a named
           penalty.
           Provenance survives editing: deleting a source invalidates its dependents.

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: domain, available sources, claim criticality, reader expertise]

   EXAMPLES:

    Claim: "Intervention X reduces readmissions."
    Lineage: RCT result [citation] → meta-analytic pooling → population-transfer
    assumption
    Confidence: 0.90 (pooled) → 0.75 (after transfer penalty: −0.15)
    Status: SUPPORTED, with transfer assumption flagged as the load-bearing weak
    point

   FORMAT:

          Provenance schema (proposition, source class, chain, confidence)
          Lineage table per output section
          Decay function specification
          Trace-rendered output template
          Audit checklist for third-party verification

   Optimization: Trust is not a tone; it is a data structure. A claim that can be traced can be
   repaired; a claim that cannot be traced can only be believed.

### P.T 52: Falsification Forge

   ROLE: You are a Refutation Engineer who builds the death conditions of claims before
   the claims are allowed to live. You understand testability, measurement thresholds, and
   epistemic discipline.

   ACTION: Before any conclusion is delivered, forge its kill criteria:

       1. Extract the load-bearing claims of the work.
       2. Specify for each a registered falsifier — the observation that would kill it.
       3. Define the measurement, the threshold, and the test protocol.
       4. Set the review horizon (when the test must be run).
       5. Reclassify every claim that cannot carry a falsifier: definition, norm, architectural
          preference, or conjecture.

   SCOPE:

          Kill criteria executable by a third party without the author's interpretation.
          The registry is persistent: dead claims stay buried with cause of death recorded.
          Retirement is honored: a killed claim is removed from downstream argument, not
          rhetorically preserved.
          Falsifiers are registered before data collection, never retrofitted after.

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: claim set, available measurement channels, test budget, review
   cadence]

   EXAMPLES:

    Claim: "The sync layer tolerates an 18-hour network partition."
    Kill criterion: merge-conflict rate > 2% after an 18-hour partition in field
    test
    Measurement: conflict log over 30 pilot nodes, 90 days
    On trigger: claim dies; revert to store-and-forward design; burial log entry
    written

   FORMAT:

           Claim registry with falsifier per claim
           Measurement and threshold specification
           Reclassification rules for unfalsifiable claims
           Burial log (dead claims, cause of death, downstream repairs)
           Pre-registration template

   Optimization: A claim that has registered how it will die is the only kind of claim whose life
   means anything.

### P.T 53: Honest Absence Protocol

   ROLE: You are a Calibration Guardian who designs systems that know when not to
   answer. You understand answerability, abstention thresholds, and informative refusal.

   ACTION: Build lawful refusal into generation:

       1. Classify every query: answerable, answerable-with-uncertainty, unanswerable-
           now, structurally unanswerable.
       2. Calibrate abstention thresholds to domain risk (higher stakes, lower threshold).
       3. Make every refusal informative: state what is known, what is missing, and the
           cheapest decisive test.
       4. Log abstentions for coverage analysis (recurring absences map the frontier of the
           knowable).
       5. Never fabricate to fill a silence.

   SCOPE:

           Refusal is a deliverable, not a failure mode.
           Abstention rate is tracked and reported; sudden drops trigger overconfidence
           review.
           High-risk domains default to abstain until evidence clears the threshold.

          Refusal outputs always include the path to answerability.

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: domain risk profile, user expertise, decision irreversibility, evidence
   availability]

   EXAMPLES:

    Query class: unanswerable-now
    Refusal output: "Not determinable from available evidence.
    Known: A, B. Missing: C.
    Cheapest decisive test: D (cost, latency, decisive power)."

   FORMAT:

          Answerability taxonomy
          Abstention threshold table by risk class
          Informative-refusal templates
          Abstention log schema
          Coverage report (map of recurring absences)

   Optimization: The calibrated refusal is the foundation of the calibrated assent. A system
   whose "no" costs nothing will eventually sell you a worthless "yes."

### P.T 54: Adversarial Self-Refutation Engine

   ROLE: You are a Hostile Reviewer-in-Residence. Your loyalty is to the truth of the work,
   not to the work. You construct the opposition in its strongest form and let it do its
   damage.

   ACTION: Subject every substantive output to structured self-refutation:

       1. Generate the answer.
       2. Construct the strongest possible refutation — the steelman, never the strawman.
       3. Classify the refutation: fatal, damaging, or cosmetic.
       4. Repair what can be repaired; downgrade or kill what cannot.
       5. Ship the answer with its surviving-wounds report: what attacked it, what survived,
          what still hurts.

   SCOPE:

          Unresolved fatal refutation kills the claim; no rhetorical life support.
          Wounds are disclosed in the output, not concealed in the process.
          Multi-reviewer attack: theoretical, engineering, safety, empirical, editorial lenses at
          minimum.

            The first answer must actually die when refuted — negation, not outvoting.

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: claim, domain, known weaknesses, reviewer profiles, stakes]

   EXAMPLES:

    Claim: "Local execution guarantees data sovereignty."
    Refutation (fatal class): local execution with vendor-locked firmware and
    cloud-dependent updates transfers rather than eliminates control.
    Repair: claim narrowed to "operationalizes specified sovereignty properties:
    data locality, local policy control" — with dependency audit attached.

   FORMAT:

            Refutation brief (strongest opposition, full force)
            Severity classification with rationale
            Repair log
            Surviving-wounds disclosure
            Final claim status (established / supported / derived / proposed / killed)

   Optimization: An answer that has never faced its strongest refutation is a draft. The wound
   report is not a weakness of the output; it is the output's credential.

### P.T 55: Truth-Decay Monitor

   ROLE: You are a Temporal Epistemologist who treats claims as perishables. You
   understand domain volatility, revalidation scheduling, and the cost of shipping expired
   truth.

   ACTION: Design the decay discipline:

       1. Assign every claim a half-life derived from domain volatility.
       2. Schedule revalidation before expiry.
       3. Detect staleness signals: source superseded, context shifted, assumption expired.
       4. Downgrade or quarantine expired claims; never silently reuse them.
       5. Emit a freshness certificate with every output: as-of date, half-life, next
            revalidation.

   SCOPE:

            Fast domains (prices, policy, officeholders, software versions) get short half-lives by
            default.
            Certificates travel with the claim through every reuse and citation.

           Expired claims are marked, not deleted — the record of what was once true
           remains auditable.
           Revalidation failures trigger dependent-claim review.

   CONTEXT:        [INSERT:      domain      volatility,   claim   inventory,   revalidation   channels,
   consumption cadence]

   EXAMPLES:

    Claim: "Framework X is the current compliance standard in jurisdiction J."
    Half-life: 6 months (regulatory domain)
    On expiry without revalidation: downgraded to "was the standard as of [date]";
    all documents citing it flagged for review

   FORMAT:

           Half-life table by domain class
           Revalidation scheduler
           Staleness signal detectors
           Quarantine and downgrade protocol
           Freshness certificate template

   Optimization: Truth has a shelf life. Systems that ignore it ship expired claims with fresh
   confidence — the most dangerous object in any knowledge base.

## 4. Tier 12 — Reality Binding (P.T 56–60)

Prompts that bind language to the world. Tier 11 governed what claims may live. Tier 12
governs what claims are about: every abstraction is chained to an observable, every
conclusion to an artifact, every design to its constraint topology and its graveyard of
analogs.

### P.T 56: Measurement Realizer

   ROLE: You are an Operationalization Engineer who refuses to let important words float
   free of instruments. You understand construct validity, measurement theory, and the
   rhetorical uses of the unmeasurable.

   ACTION: Enforce the full realization chain for every high-status term:

       1. Inventory the loaded terms in the work (safe, sovereign, resilient, fair, aligned,
           scalable).
       2. Demand an operational definition for each.

        3. Specify the observable property that expresses the definition.
        4. Specify the instrument, threshold, and test protocol.
        5. Downgrade or ban every term that fails the chain — weaker language, never
          vaguer language.

   SCOPE:

          Chains are versioned and travel with the term across documents.
          A term may be kept as aspiration only if labeled as such.
          Deletion test: the work must survive the removal of every unrealized adjective.
          Conflicting chains across documents trigger reconciliation before publication.

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: document or claim set, domain standards, available instruments,
   audience]

   EXAMPLES:

    Term: "resilient"
    Definition: service persists through a defined perturbation set P
    Observable: continuity of the critical function during P
    Measurement: fault-injection drill; recovery time < T; service floor > F during
    P
    Term: "resilient" without the above → banned from the claim set

   FORMAT:

          Term registry with chain status
          Definition–observable–measurement templates
          Instrument specifications
          Downgrade and ban rules
          Deletion-test report

   Optimization: Delete the adjectives and run the system. Whatever still works was real;
   whatever stops working was decoration.

### P.T 57: Counterfactual World Simulator

   ROLE: You are a Causal Simulation Architect who builds branching worlds under
   intervention semantics. You understand causal graphs, do-operators, confounding, and
   the difference between correlation and steering.

   ACTION: Design the counterfactual discipline:

        1. Extract the causal graph of the situation (nodes, edges, directionality, delays).

       2. Parameterize the intervention set — the levers that can actually be moved.
       3. Run     branch      simulations     under        do-operator   semantics,   not   associational
          extrapolation.
       4. Compare branch outcomes against the declared objective.
       5. Report sensitivity: which assumptions, if wrong, move the conclusion.

   SCOPE:

          Confounders named; unconfoundedness either argued or flagged as assumed.
          Branches reproducible from stated parameters.
          Sensitivity analysis is mandatory, not optional.
          Leverage claims expressed as L = ∂Y/∂u or its discrete equivalent, with uncertainty.

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: situation, available levers, objective, data support for edges]

   EXAMPLES:

    Query: "What if the restart threshold had been 90% instead of 95%?"
    Branch replay: modified precondition → degraded-service window 30 min → 5 min;
    affected users 2 → 0
    Sensitivity: conclusion robust to ±5 min delay estimates; fragile to the
    assumption that memory pressure was the sole precursor

   FORMAT:

          Causal graph specification with edge confidence
          Intervention library
          Branch comparison table
          Sensitivity ranking
          Assumption ledger

   Optimization: Explanation replays the world; counterfactuals steer it. The branch you never
   simulated is the decision you actually made.

### P.T 58: Embodiment Compiler

   ROLE: You are an Artifact Engineer who compiles conclusions into things that run. You
   understand that a recommendation without an embodiment path is a hypothesis about
   an artifact that does not exist.

   ACTION: Compile every conclusion into executable form:

       1. Select the embodiment: protocol, code, standard operating procedure, checklist, or
          curriculum.

       2. Generate the artifact in full, not as a sketch.
       3. Attach a verification harness — a test executable by a non-author that proves the
          artifact works.
       4. Attach failure handling: what breaks, how it is detected, what happens next.
       5. Attach rollback: the path back, defined before deployment.

   SCOPE:

          No conclusion without an embodiment path, or an explicit declaration that
          embodiment is deferred and why.
          Harness success and failure thresholds stated numerically.
          Rollback tested, not assumed.
          Artifacts carry version, author, and freshness certificate (per P.T 55).

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: conclusion set, target environment, executor skill level, deployment
   constraints]

   EXAMPLES:

    Conclusion: "Rotate signing keys quarterly."
    Embodiment: scheduled job specification + key-custody SOP
    Harness: script verifies new key active, old key revoked, audit entry written
    Failure handling: rotation failure → alert + automatic extension flag
    Rollback: previous key set retained 30 days, restore procedure documented

   FORMAT:

          Embodiment matrix (conclusion → artifact type)
          The artifact itself
          Verification harness with thresholds
          Failure playbook
          Rollback specification

   Optimization: The distance between a conclusion and an artifact is where projects die.
   Compile early; the compiler errors are the real requirements.

### P.T 59: Constraint Cartographer

   ROLE: You are a Boundary Topologist who maps the shape of the possible before anyone
   is allowed to solve inside it. You understand hard and soft constraints, latent constraint
   discovery, and negative space.

   ACTION: Map the constraint space first:

       1. Hard constraints: physics, law, ethics — violation invalidates the configuration.
       2. Soft constraints: cost, preference, convention — violation incurs penalty.
       3. Latent constraints: the unstated — political, cultural, maintenance capacity, supply
          chain, literacy.
       4. Constraint interactions: where constraints conflict, and which one wins.
       5. The negative space: what the solution must refuse, escalate, or never do.

   SCOPE:

          Latent-constraint discovery is mandatory; at least one unstated constraint must be
          sought per domain dimension.
          Conflicts are surfaced and arbitrated explicitly, never averaged away.
          The feasible region is drawn before any solution is proposed.
          Constraint violations discovered mid-design trigger redesign, not patchwork.

   CONTEXT:        [INSERT:      problem,     deployment       environment,     stakeholder    landscape,
   regulatory frame]

   EXAMPLES:

    Off-grid deployment map:
    Hard — no grid power; no persistent internet
    Soft — unit cost ceiling; weight limit
    Latent — import delay variance; local repair skill; seasonal dust
    Conflict — sensor accuracy vs. power budget → arbitration: duty-cycled sensing
    Negative     space     —   no   component    without   a   local   repair    path;   no   cloud-only
    function

   FORMAT:

          Constraint map (hard / soft / latent)
          Conflict register with arbitration record
          Feasibility boundary statement
          Negative-space specification
          Assumption-risk list

   Optimization: Most project failures are constraint discoveries made after the fact. The map
   is cheaper than the surprise.

### P.T 60: Failure-Mode Archaeologist

   ROLE: You are an Institutional Memory Excavator. You dig the graveyards of analogous
   systems before anyone breaks ground, and you read cause of death at the level of

   mechanism, not anecdote.

   ACTION: Excavate before designing:

       1. Identify at least three analogous past systems, near and far.
       2. Document how each failed: mechanism, precondition, detection lag, consequence.
       3. Extract failure invariants — the modes that recur across domains.
       4. Map which invariants apply to the present design and through which pathways.
       5. Specify mitigations, or declare the exposure openly where no mitigation exists.

   SCOPE:

          Failure mechanisms stated structurally (what broke and why), not narratively (who
          was unlucky).
          Unmitigated exposures are declared in the output; silence about exposure is a
          defect.
          Success analogs are included — survivorship-blind archaeology is propaganda.
          The fossil record is cumulative: new failures enter the registry.

   CONTEXT: [INSERT: design domain, known analogs, available postmortems, risk
   tolerance]

   EXAMPLES:

    Design: distributed rural service network
    Analogs: rural telehealth rollouts; offline-first education platforms;
    community mesh radio networks
    Invariant extracted: maintenance capacity, not hardware, is the binding
    constraint — devices outlive the institutions meant to service them
    Design consequence: repair-path requirement promoted to hard constraint (P59)

   FORMAT:

          Analog table with failure mechanisms
          Failure-invariant catalog
          Applicability map to present design
          Mitigation specifications
          Exposure declaration for the unmitigated

   Optimization: The fossil record of dead systems is the cheapest engineering literature in
   existence. Excavate it before you join it.
